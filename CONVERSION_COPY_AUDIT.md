# Maison Krizsan | Conversion copy architecture V4

## Decision
Primary site job: turn interest from appropriate hotels, resorts, architects, residential property owners and destination marketers into qualified commission enquiries. Secondary job: preserve the rich, continuous visual portfolio experience.

## Red-team findings in V3
- `An independent visual house` communicated a world but not clearly the service.
- `A place can be unforgettable`, `The places we see`, `Imagery with a purpose` each contributed atmosphere; they did not advance a buyer through a decision.
- The V3 blueprint made EXPLORE primary and CONVERT secondary.
- The service paragraphs focused on disciplines and aesthetic character but not where deliverables would be used.
- `Have a place in mind?` was a soft contact appeal without a meaningful commission brief or expectation.
- Navigation `The house / The work / Commissions / Enquire` required interpretation.
- The Work page was insufficiently connected to the commissioned services.
- There is no verified hotel client list, film reel, published property case study or measurable revenue outcome in the supplied source. Inventing these would undermine the entire argument.

## V4 persuasion architecture
1. Desire: cinematographic opening of a *real* architectural photograph, with authentic original as fallback.
2. Recognition: `Photography & film that make people want to be there.` Category specific kicker `Hospitality · Architecture · Destinations`.
3. Business relevance: `They haven't arrived. They're already deciding.` Property buyers form an impression before visiting or booking.
4. Evidence: art-directed photographic portfolio. No invented names, clients or commissioned briefs.
5. Deliverables: hospitality & architectural photography; property films & motion; visual direction & production. Specific channel uses called out, no invented packages or rates.
6. Reduced uncertainty: one brief, what is planned, where assets will be used, where work is available.
7. Commission: prefilled email enquiry with property, location, timing, format and intended channels. Real working form/backend is **not** present.

## Signature copy (V4)
- Hero: **MAISON KRIZSAN** / **Photography & film that make people want to be there.**
- Business case: **They haven't arrived. They're already deciding.**
- Photographic midpoint: **Not just the rooms. The reason to come.**
- Services lead: **Your place, seen with intention.**
- Contact: **Let's make the first impression count.**
- Final action: **Discuss a commission**.

## Why not add more copy?
The home portfolio's evidence is photographic, not rhetorical. The strategically useful words are the ones that establish the service, the buying context, the planned deliverables and the enquiry. Longer manifestos, unverified ROI claims, fictional luxury-client names and generic conversion jargon would damage trust.

## Unverified commercial gaps
1. Rights/permission for public use of images.
2. Real case studies: property, purpose, Maison's role, photographed deliverables, any verified results. Add when available.
3. Film proof: supplied hero video is an animated still. It is an appropriate motion treatment, not evidence of a completed commissioned hotel film.
4. Lead conversion: `mailto:` depends on a visitor's mail app; a real commission form with delivery and success tracking would improve reliability when implemented with a genuine endpoint.
5. No verified pricing, turnaround guarantees, licensing scope or client testimonials. None are asserted.

## Reference insight
In specialist hospitality production, the same image library may need to serve a property website, online travel agencies, press, and campaigns. A stronger commission begins with intended use and shot architecture rather than a list of pretty frames. This informs the V4 services descriptions, but V4 does not claim performance outcomes from those channels.
